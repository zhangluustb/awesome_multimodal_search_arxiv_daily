| Data-Void Exploits in Web-Searching LLMs | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30365) | Web-searching Large Language Models (LLMs) have changed how users seek online information: instead of sifting through ranked lists of links, users read synthesized answers. This shift creates a new LLM vulnerability: a data-void exploit. Disinformers publish their narratives in search spaces where relevant and reliable content is scarce, i.e. data voids, then rely on the LLM's web-search to retrieve and then use, cite, or list those pages in its responses to data-void seeking prompts. Unlike classical search, where users see ranking cues and competing results, LLMs can obscure when an answer relies on sparse, unverified, or adversarial sources. We study this threat by varying the user's prompt tone - skeptical, excited, or neutral - and LLM choice - ChatGPT, Gemini, or Sonar Pro - as well as three attacker controls. The attacker can use one of five known strategies to construct and fill a data void with their narrative, host their content at varying levels of visibility from well-established, indexed domains to less established ones, and include generative-optimization engine features thought to boost citing by web-searching LLMs. We find that data-void exploits can succeed with only a single attacker controlled page. Across all conditions, attacker-content appears in LLM responses 31% of the time. User controls offer limited protection: while skeptical prompts slightly reduce success and slightly increase flagging or warnings in LLM responses, we find no evidence of large effects of prompt tone or model choice on success rates, although smaller effects cannot be ruled out. Overall, our results establish data voids as a practical inference-time attack surface for manipulating web-searching LLMs through strategically placed web content and highlight the urgent need for defenses against such exploits. | Michele Guerra, Wuyeh Jobe, Sanjana Nambiar, Christina P\"opper, Azza Abouzied |
| Compiling Autoregressive LLM Control into Policy-Exact Parallel Inference for Hierarchical RAG | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30664) | Large language models increasingly act as controllers in structured decision pipelines, yet their decisions are still executed by autoregressive text decoding. Hierarchical RAG is one such setting: at each frontier of a document tree, an LLM commits to $b$ of $k$ sections under an input-dependent action grammar, executed as either failure-prone free-form JSON or reliable but strictly serial grammar-constrained generation. We show that this serialization is unnecessary for bounded-horizon control: the reachable future forms a shallow action-prefix tree, which we compile --- one root prefill plus one tree-causal probe evaluate every prefix-conditioned action distribution with serial decoding's tokens, positions, and causal ancestry. A prefix-state lemma makes compiled control policy-exact in exact arithmetic: the same policy with a two-forward critical path and zero output tokens. One further probe pass certifies the exact order-marginalized MAP set. Across four document-disjoint splits and two scales on the primary MetaX device, compiled control matches the serial policy on $84$--$89\%$ of questions --- every instrumented divergence a floating-point near-tie --- and cuts control latency by $35$--$47\%$ versus token-wise serial decoding and $6$--$7\%$ versus an idealized chunked engine; an H800 repetition preserves the ordering. | Zehao Lin, Qianfeng Shen, Xixuan Hao, Shaobo Cui, Chunyu Li, Kai Chen, Bo Tang, Zhenjun Wang, Jin Yan, Zhiyu Li, Feiyu Xiong |
| POI Recommendation with LLM-Augmented Multi-Graph Learning and Contrastive Alignment | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16407) | Point-of-interest (POI) recommendation models based on graph neural networks achieve strong performance by propagating collaborative signals over user-item interactions, yet they struggle with the cold-start problem, where items with few or no interactions are not represented. In this paper, we propose LLM-augmented Multi-Graph Contrastive Learning (LLM-MGCL), a multi-graph neural network that uses semantic and spatial information about items to extend the LightGCN backbone with two auxiliary item-item graphs: a semantic graph constructed from sentence embeddings of LLM-generated photo summaries and keywords, and a geographic graph derived from Haversine distances between business locations. Item embeddings are propagated over all three graphs in parallel, fused additively, and aligned across views through a bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive objective that connects behavioral, semantic, and spatial representations of the same items. Experiments on the Yelp Multimodal Recommendation Dataset show that LLM-MGCL outperforms classical collaborative filtering, matrix factorization, and non-contrastive graph neural network baselines. It improves Recall@20 by 48.5% and NDCG@20 by 59.0% over LightGCN, while trailing the strongest contrastive baseline, Self-supervised Graph Learning (SGL), slightly in aggregate. A popularity-stratified evaluation shows that LLM-MGCL achieves the highest recall on cold and warm items, whereas SGL remains better on popular items. An ablation study reveals that the cross-view contrastive alignment (CA) is the primary driver of the aggregate gains. Our results suggest that externally grounded, LLM-derived item knowledge can partially compensate for missing collaborative signal and mitigate the item cold-start problem in POI recommendation. | Burak Tamer, Wolfram H\"opken, Zehui Wang, Thomas Bayer |
| Semantics Delivery Network: Rethinking Web Retrieval Infrastructure for LLM Agents | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22486) | Large language models (LLMs) increasingly ground their answers in proprietary data and live web content through both single-shot retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and multi-turn agentic search. Yet the web path they use was built for human readers: search services return URLs and snippets ranked by generic relevance, and content delivery networks (CDNs) cache URL-addressed objects without knowing which passage an agent needs. LLMs instead consume short, semantically coherent passages - hereafter, chunks - selected for downstream utility rather than similarity alone, retrieve statefully across reasoning turns, and, as uncoordinated streams, repeatedly fetch, clean, chunk, and embed the same content. We argue that semantic chunk retrieval should become a first-class network-delivery abstraction, and propose the Semantics Delivery Network (SemDN): an origin-authorized, hierarchical substrate that indexes, searches, and caches web content at chunk granularity, serving clients and origins alike. SemDN is more than finer-grained caching: it must estimate missing or stale evidence without an explicit miss signal, let clients choose retrieval representations that origins need not see, and share state among clients with the same representation. Preliminary results show that page-first retrieval processes two to three orders of magnitude more content than agents consume, that agentic queries exhibit task-local chunk reuse invisible to URL caches, and that chunk delivery improves answer quality per context token. We organize an open research agenda around shareable retrieval state, hierarchical caching and refresh under coverage risk, and deployability, including incentives, privacy, and verifiability. Our code is available at: https://github.com/sarendis56/SemDN_HotNets26 | Peichun Hua, Yunming Xiao |
