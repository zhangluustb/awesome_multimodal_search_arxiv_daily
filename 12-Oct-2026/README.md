| SPARQL-LLM in the TEXT2SPARQL 2026 Challenge | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13648) | Querying distributed data represented as Knowledge Graphs (KGs) via SPARQL remains a difficult task, requiring fluency in query syntax, endpoint specifications, and schema details. Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) systems address this by automatically translating natural language into SPARQL, and recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have made this translation increasingly feasible. In this paper, we describe SPARQL-LLM, our open-source KGQA system, in the context of the TEXT2SPARQL 2026 challenge. In this challenge, SPARQL-LLM ranks first overall among eight competing approaches across all datasets, achieving up to 59% higher accuracy than the second-best approach and supporting multilingual question answering in English, German, and Spanish. SPARQL-LLM is publicly released at https://github.com/sib-swiss/sparql-llm and deployed in production over decentralized bioinformatics KGs at https://www.expasy.org/chat. | Panayiotis Smeros, Vincent Emonet, Ruijie Wang, Ana-Claudia Sima, Tarcisio Mendes de Farias |
| Tracing and Taming Hallucinated Terms via Subsequence-Association Filtering and Breadth-Aware Selection for LLM Query Expansion | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13810) | Large language models (LLMs) are now a standard source of query expansion terms but they hallucinate. They inject non existent entities and off-topic terms that add ranking noise and can hurt strong retrievers. Existing remedies such as corpus grounding, mutual verification and preference alignment treat hallucinated terms as an undifferentiated nuisance to be filtered by agreement heuristics, without a mechanistic account of why a given term appears or a principled criterion for how many terms to keep. We propose Subsequence-Association Filtered, breadth-aware query expansion (SAFE-QE), a training-free, retriever agnostic method that draws on the subsequence-association account of hallucination. We score each candidate expansion term by its corpus conditioned cross-context reproducibility, terms that reappear under randomised query contexts yet lack support in the pseudo-relevant set are flagged as products of dominant hallucinatory associations and down-weighted. Drawing on the characterisation of language generation as a trade-off between consistency (no hallucination) and breadth (coverage), we cast term selection as constrained coverage maximisation over the pseudo-relevant set, keeping a set that is approximately broad but consistent and stable across resampling. We also introduce the Hallucinated Expansion Term Rate, a query side faithfulness metric that counts injected expansion terms no relevant document supports. We evaluate our approach on benchmarked TREC and BEIR collections. Our SAFE-QE is statistically indistinguishable from generative and corpus-steered expansion on nDCG@10 while injecting fewer unsupported terms 4.3 per query against 27.8 for Query2doc and 57.5 for HyDE and on a strong learned sparse retriever, where every expansion method regresses. Faithfulness is therefore available at no measurable cost in ranking quality. | Amit Kumar Jaiswal, Sagar Uprety, Prayag Tiwari |
| Locate, Judge, Read: Role-Specialized Tool Agents over a Knowledge-Point Index for Open-Domain Multimodal Document QA | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13128) | Question answering over large heterogeneous document collections requires first finding the right passages and then reading them, yet current retrieval-augmented agents give one model both kinds of tools and let it schedule itself. We present KnowForge, a pipeline separated by job: a hybrid-retrieval knowledge-point index; a Locator agent that can only search; a code-scheduled bridge judge that forces the missing second hop; a typed, size-bounded evidence pool that merges every search's ranking; a Deep Reader agent that can only read text, images and tables; and a synthesizer with a format checker. With a self-hosted Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (3B active), KnowForge reaches F1 47.5 on the full open-domain M3DocVQA (2,441 questions, 3,368 PDFs), +11.0 over the published M3DocRAG system and +7.1 over one-shot retrieve-and-answer on the same index and model, and 53.5 with DeepSeek-V4-Flash. On MultiHop-RAG it reaches 73.5 accuracy, above the strongest published RAG and GraphRAG results (71.2); on the single-document MMLongBench-Doc it lifts the same model from 34.8 to 47.0, level with GPT-4o reading page images (46.3). Per-job ablations on the full benchmarks attribute the gain: the Deep Reader is worth 3.8, 9.8 and 0 points on the three corpora, tracking how visual each is; merging both agents into one with all tools and twice the rounds loses 1.9 F1 because it keeps searching instead of reading; a round-budget sweep shows a step at four Locator rounds and a plateau beyond; query latency scales with model calls rather than corpus size. We serve the three indexed benchmarks as read-only knowledge bases (free registration) and release all prompts and the runner code: https://github.com/metask-ai/knowforge-bench | Wenlin Lin, Fanzhe Wei |
| Imagine Before Acting: Prospective Skill Identification for LLM Agents | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01642) | Effective skill identification is crucial for LLM agents to reuse task-solving experience for complex task execution, which however remains challenging as agents must accurately identify useful skills from a large skill gallery and invoke them on demand. Existing studies have explored various strategies for skill retrieval and invocation, but largely overlook the natural discrepancy between task descriptions and skill documents. To be specific, our empirical study reveals that task descriptions mainly specify task objectives, whereas skill documents describe the required capabilities and procedural guidance for achieving the objectives. Such a so-called Task--Skill Misalignment (TSM) problem would cause desirable skills to be neglected during retrieval or remain unselected during invocation, thus hindering task execution. As a remedy, inspired by human prospective cognition, we propose SkillDreamer, a prospective skill identification framework to mitigate TSM. In brief, SkillDreamer first infers execution-required capabilities and imagines their realizations as pseudo skills, thereby translating task objectives into execution-oriented guidance for skill retrieval. As execution proceeds, SkillDreamer reevaluates the capabilities required by emerging subtasks to determine which skills should be invoked, thereby aligning skill invocation with evolving execution needs. Extensive experiments on three representative agent skill benchmarks not only verify the effectiveness of SkillDreamer in mitigating TSM, but also demonstrate its generalizability across diverse retrievers and LLM agents. The code will be released upon acceptance. | Shuo Liu, Yutong Yang, Haohao Xiao, Mouxing Yang, Xi Peng |
| Claim-Gated Source-Risk Auditing for Generative Search | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29145) | A generative search answer can cite a supported passage yet omit a source relationship that changes its interpretation. We specify a claim-gated audit of the query-source-answer tuple. An omission is resolved only when relationship evidence, answer adoption, materiality, and disclosure are all observed; incomplete evidence remains unresolved rather than being treated as independence. The specification separates this endpoint from citation support and review priority, and binds decisions to versioned evidence spans. A reference checker makes the record contract executable. On an exhaustive synthetic suite, it reproduces all 81 three-state predicate combinations and rejects 192 deliberately malformed records. Common-guard baselines and predicate ablations isolate endpoint logic from missing-evidence handling, while controlled transitions check support separation and evidence removal. These are finite contract-conformance results, not detector accuracy or evidence of improved user outcomes. We define the independent annotation, held-out evaluation, and paired utility tests still required to establish semantic validity and deployment benefit. | Kainan Zhou, Chuhong Xu, Gangzhen Qian, Zhaoyi Li |
