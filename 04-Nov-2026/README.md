| IntentGEO: Intent-aware Generative Engine Optimization for Recommendation | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04225) | Large language models (LLMs) increasingly shape recommendations across chatbots, e-commerce, and user-generated content (UGC) platforms, creating demand for Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) to improve item visibility in generated responses. Existing GEO studies are mainly content-centric, optimizing visibility under general-purpose queries. However, a shared item description usually serves users with different needs and decision goals, resulting in its visibility gains being dependent on the request. To investigate how changing intent affects visibility, we formulate IntentGEO to evaluate shared rewrites across need specificity and decision progression. We revisit existing GEO methods and find that their visibility gains vary across user intents. More critically, learning-based prompt optimization can underperform its handwritten seed on average. Tracing these updates reveals prompt drift from explicit recommendation instructions toward generic promotional tactics. To address this drift, we propose AMO (Anchored Meta-Optimization), which preserves recommendation instructions in a fixed core while learning appended strategies from citation feedback. Across four domains and ten intent states, AMO improves average citation-rank gain by 35% over the strongest prior learning-based baseline and leads on nine states. Ablations show that learned additions improve on the fixed core alone and that AMO outperforms full-prompt optimization initialized from that core. It also achieves the highest gains in an agentic recommendation pipeline and, after separate adaptation, on Doubao, a commercially deployed, closed-source online assistant. These findings highlight user intent as a key dimension of GEO evaluation and support anchored adaptation across recommendation needs and decision stages. | Runlin Lei, Shiqi Zhang, Xiaokui Xiao, Zhewei Wei |
| ReuseGR: Offline--Online Decoupled Generative Retrieval via Adaptive Prefix Reuse | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04259) | Larger generative retrieval models can improve retrieval quality, but their decoding cost makes low-latency serving difficult. When different users submit the same query, generating complete Semantic IDs (SIDs) for every request repeats computation, while their interaction histories call for different results. The challenge is to reuse large-model computation across requests while keeping retrieval personalized.
  We propose \textbf{ReuseGR}, which separates query-level candidate planning from request-level personalized completion. A 7B offline planner generates SID prefix sets shared across requests with the same query. A 0.5B online model uses each request's history to select prefixes and generate the remaining tokens. ReuseCut chooses a cache depth for each query based on historical coverage, balancing computation reuse and candidate coverage. To strengthen online completion, we transfer 7B suffix-scoring knowledge to 0.5B through path-level knowledge distillation (Path KD). BudgetCut then selects scored branches to improve final top-100 recall under a fixed branch budget. Its selection policy is initialized by on-policy distillation (OPD) and refined through group-relative policy optimization (GRPO).
  On an industrial search-advertising dataset with approximately 2M query--item records and 3.38M catalog items, ReuseGR improves query-level HR@100 by 3.83 percentage points and reduces mean online latency by 38.9\% relative to an all-online 0.5B baseline when prefixes are prepared. It achieves HR@100 comparable to an all-online 7B baseline with 57.7\% lower online latency. A 14-day user-level online A/B test against the 0.5B baseline shows relative gains of 0.93\% in click-through rate and 1.05\% in revenue per thousand requests. | Xujing Wang, Yufeng Chen, Boxuan Zhang, Chao Wei, Cai Xu, Jie Zhao, Weiru Zhang, Wei Zhao, Ziyu Guan |
| OneTrans-GSR: A Generative Slate Ranking Paradigm Unifying Reranking and Mix-Ranking with One Autoregressive Transformer | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04720) | Industrial recommender systems allocate substantial computation to pointwise candidate scoring, yet separate reranking and mix-ranking stages still assemble the final exposure slate using hand-crafted rules and specialized low-capacity models. Mix-ranking combines product cards, short videos, livestreams, and advertising cards supplied by distinct upstream business channels. Although these rules and models encode valuable domain knowledge, they are inherently limited in leveraging additional data and computation. They constrain the combinatorial space of candidate slates and determine objective trade-offs through manual tuning. To enable scalable and effective slate generation, we reformulate reranking and mix-ranking as a unified learning problem whose performance can improve with computation. Specifically, we instantiate this formulation across the reranking and mix-ranking stages and propose OneTrans-GSR, a single decoder-only policy that extends our deployed OneTrans backbone to generate the exposure slate end-to-end. During training, a reward model evaluates complete slates and guides reinforcement learning toward slate-level and long-term objectives. During decoding, business constraints are explicitly enforced. Offline evaluation shows that slate quality consistently improves as reward-model and policy compute increase. Deployed in a large-scale industrial recommendation system, OneTrans-GSR mixes heterogeneous content types within a unified slate. In online A/B tests, it improves GMV/u by 2.63%, advertiser value by 2.06%, and repurchase rate by 2.03%, while reducing end-to-end latency across the reranking and mix-ranking stages by 13%. These results mark a paradigm shift in slate generation: moving beyond human-engineered heuristics toward a general learned policy creates a scalable path for further algorithmic optimization and compute-driven improvement. | Feng Cheng, Yu-Hsuan Huang, Fengyi Wang, Chaofan Zhou, Zhennan Zhu, Huan He, Bo Zhang, Ruoqi Zhang, Zhanfeng Mo, Ziwei Liu, Lanyi Li, Yifeng Pan, Junhua Long, Ziqiu Zhou, Yue Lin, Shaowei Liu |
| Dataset Recommendation for Heterogeneous and Dynamic Scholarly Graphs via Multimodal Representation Learning | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05071) | Researchers struggle to find relevant datasets amid the growing volume of data across repositories and scholarly publications. Manual dataset retrieval is time-consuming and often fails to identify valuable resources that are not easily discoverable through direct links or popularity metrics. Dataset recommendation, which aims to identify datasets relevant to a paper, author, or query, has become essential for improving dataset discoverability and reuse. However, this task remains challenging because Scholarly Knowledge Graphs (SKGs) are large-scale, heterogeneous, dynamic, and incomplete. Although openly available SKGs organize scientific knowledge by linking scholarly outcomes, they often lack meaningful connections between datasets, leaving them isolated and underutilized. These graphs contain millions of evolving relations but still suffer from missing or inaccurate metadata, duplicate records, and weak dataset integration. This work presents the Multimodal Scholarly Attention Network (MM-SAN), a heterogeneous multimodal graph representation learning method for recommending datasets relevant to research publications within real-world SKGs. MM-SAN combines topology-aware and text-based embeddings, enabling effective recommendations even when textual metadata is limited or absent. It operates in transductive, semi-inductive, and fully inductive settings, adapting to evolving SKGs without requiring retraining. Experiments show that MM-SAN outperforms state-of-the-art methods, providing a robust and scalable solution for dataset recommendation. | Ornella Irrera, Matteo Lissandrini, Daniele Dell'Aglio, Gianmaria Silvello |
| HARP: Training-Free Dual-Profile Agentic Communication for LLM-Based Recommendation | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05319) | In LLM-based recommendation, memory acts as a user-level agent in a memory-augmented backbone. Yet users and items remain asymmetric: only the user is given a structured profile; it enters a bandwidth-limited reranker prompt; and it is frozen after warmup. We recast LLM-based recommendation as an agentic-communication process in four stages: BUILD a self-description, TRANSMIT it along the collaborative graph, UNDERSTAND the other side, and SYNC it from the next interaction. This formulation reveals three unresolved communication challenges: the item side never produces a comparable self-description (one-sided BUILD); profile-level signals enter only the bandwidth-limited reranker prompt rather than retrieval, where they would not compete for context tokens (prompt-trapped UNDERSTAND); and the user's description is frozen after warmup with no principled update rule (absent SYNC). We instantiate the missing stages as HARP (Homophily-aware Agentic Recommendation via dual Profiles), a training-free, three-operator addition to a frozen LLM backbone: a rule-extracted symmetric item profile (zero extra LLM calls), a channel-decomposable profile-level homophily H(phi_u, phi_i) consumed at retrieval time rather than inside the reranker prompt, and a single-pass reflective update realising in-context profile evolution without any gradient step. On four public benchmarks (Yelp, Books, MovieTV, Goodreads), HARP lifts Hit@1 over the strongest published baseline by 6.9%-24.0%, with family-wise Holm-corrected p < 1e-9 on every dataset. Ablations and a per-case attribution study show that the operators contribute complementary gains, with the retrieval-side gate as the main source; we also report the trade-off introduced by the reflective pass. We formulate agentic communication and provide a training-free instantiation. Code is available at https://github.com/Jove-Zhen/HARP. | Zhen Tao, Xun Zhou, Xuhui Chen, Ziyue Qiao, Qingqiang Sun |
| ZeroGR: Filtering-First Constrained Decoding for Generative Recommendation | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04715) | Generative recommendation is gaining traction in production, retrieving items by generating their identifiers one token at a time. The generated sequences must identify relevant catalog items, so constrained decoding couples relevance scoring with validity filtering. Because most possible sequences are invalid, scoring before filtering wastes computation on candidates that will be discarded. Our evaluation shows that filtering first can avoid this work with negligible impact on recommendation quality. We present ZeroGR, a filtering-first constrained decoding system for generative recommendation. ZeroGR uses a step-sliced filtering index to reduce GPU memory use by tailoring data representations to each decoding step. Its execution engine adopts shared-slot execution to accelerate data exchange across stages and hybrid projection to adapt computation to workload patterns, thereby reducing execution overhead. During index refreshes, ZeroGR applies layout-guided streaming construction to speed up updates and limit serving interference. Extensive evaluation shows that ZeroGR accelerates the filtering and scoring pipeline by up to $9.9\times$ over representative baselines while reducing GPU index memory use and accelerating index reconstruction. | Xiangrui Yang, Jiawei Yang, Liang Zeng, Daqian Ding, Ziqian Liu, Bing Tian, Cheng Peng, Yunfeng Zhao, Fangyu Zhang, Jiaqiang Liu, Xiao Liang, Yiming Qiu |
| Factorized MLLM Embeddings for Composed Image Retrieval | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05193) | Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) aims to retrieve a target image given a reference image and a modification text. Although recent approaches leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown significant progress, we argue that the MLLM's compositional understanding remains underutilized. This stems from directly mapping composed queries into a retrieval embedding via contrastive supervision alone, without explicitly resolving the intended visual modifications. In this work, we introduce Factorized MLLM Embeddings (FactEmbed), which decouples compositional query comprehension from building discriminative embeddings. Rather than directly projecting the composed query into a single embedding without internal reasoning, FactEmbed employs a set of factorized latents that simulate the intended visual modifications via an intermediate target imagination objective. This process encourages the latents to ground into target semantics before forming discriminative representations for retrieval. Subsequently, to propagate these factorized representations throughout the model for retrieval, we optimize a factor-level contrastive loss while distilling the decomposed representations into a deeper attention layer of the MLLM. Finally, for efficient inference, we retain the [EOS] token to establish a global-level embedding on top of these factorized latents, while we exploit the mean-pooled factorized latents to rerank a short candidate list at negligible cost, as both are produced by the same forward pass. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that FactEmbed outperforms prior works across CIR benchmarks. | WonJun Moon, Seongchan Kim, Dahun Kim, Seungryong Kim |
| MuonRec: Shifting the Optimizer Paradigm Beyond Adam in Scalable Generative Recommendation | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00416) | Recommender systems (RecSys) are increasingly emphasizing scaling, leveraging larger architectures and more interaction data to improve personalization. Yet, despite the optimizer's pivotal role in training, modern RecSys pipelines almost universally default to Adam/AdamW, with limited scrutiny of whether these choices are truly optimal for recommendation. In this work, we revisit optimizer design for scalable recommendation and introduce MuonRec, the first framework that brings the recently proposed Muon optimizer to RecSys training. Muon performs orthogonalized momentum updates for 2D weight matrices via Newton-Schulz iteration, promoting diverse update directions and improving optimization efficiency. We develop an open-source training recipe for recommendation models and evaluate it across both traditional sequential recommenders and modern generative recommenders. Extensive experiments demonstrate that MuonRec reduces converged training steps by an average of 32.4\% while simultaneously improving final ranking quality. Specifically, MuonRec yields consistent relative gains in NDCG@10, averaging 12.6\% across all settings, with particularly pronounced improvements in generative recommendation models. These results consistently outperform strong Adam/AdamW baselines, positioning Muon as a promising new optimizer standard for RecSys training. Our code is available. | Rong Shan, Aofan Yu, Bo Chen, Kuo Cai, Qiang Luo, Ruiming Tang, Han Li, Weiwen Liu, Weinan Zhang, Jianghao Lin |
| RecoAtlas: A Benchmark and RL Environment for LLM Recommendation Agents Optimizing Set-Level Utility | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18805) | LLM recommender agents increasingly produce structured reports: sets of items accompanied by natural-language justifications. Yet existing benchmarks often focus on reranking small candidate sets or judging reports primarily by semantic plausibility, providing limited support for evaluating and training agents based on set-level utility. We introduce Recommender Atlas (Agentic Tasks for Learning and Assessment in Shopping), or RecoAtlas, a benchmark and reinforcement learning environment grounded in user behavior. RecoAtlas provides a shopping toolkit for product retrieval, complementarity, and diversity. Given a shopping query, an agent uses these tools to generate a structured recommendation report. Behavioral reference sets support exact-recovery evaluation and the training of a learned bundle-level reward that supplies feedback for policy optimization with RL. Its controlled tool environment exposes agents to semantic and behavior-aligned tools, enabling systematic analysis of how LLM reasoning, tool signals, and tool-use policies contribute to performance. Across controlled experiments, performance scales with model capacity and test-time compute, and improves with stronger tools, while semantic plausibility does not necessarily translate into behavior-grounded utility. RecoAtlas provides a foundation for training and evaluating shopping assistants to move beyond semantic plausibility toward coherent, behaviorally grounded recommendation sets. | Imad Aouali, Flavian Vasile, Clijo Jose, Otmane Sakhi, Alexandre Gilotte, Benjamin Heymann |
