| Controllable Generative Recommendation via Guided Token Refinement | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28642) | Generative recommendation over discrete Semantic IDs has become a powerful paradigm for item retrieval. Real-world systems, however, frequently require real-time controllability to balance accuracy against objectives such as diversity. Existing autoregressive methods generate tokens strictly from left to right. This fixed direction locks in early decisions, preventing holistic planning and making control difficult. Consequently, direct interventions often break the semantic structure, producing non-existent items. To address this, we propose ReGen, a framework that shifts from sequential decoding to parallel iterative refinement. ReGen reformulates generation as a mask-filling task, predicting all tokens simultaneously rather than one by one. This global view allows us to apply control signals directly to tokens with high-level semantics without retraining the model. To ensure that decoded candidates map to real items, we introduce a topology-constrained tree that iteratively corrects low-confidence predictions while strictly enforcing valid codebook paths during beam-based inference. Experiments show that ReGen provides a favorable accuracy-diversity trade-off under moderate guidance while maintaining high validity of generated Semantic IDs. The source code is available at: https://github.com/zhang-haichao/ReGen. | Haichao Zhang, Zhixuan Liang, Chong Zhang, Zixi Chen, Wen Wang, Jia Wang |
| Reasoning-Search-Augmented LLMs: An Operational Taxonomy, Benchmark Analysis and Controlled Evidence Synthesis | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29189) | Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate impressive emergent reasoning capabilities, yet they remain constrained by fixed model parameters and finite context windows. Even when augmented with static retrieval, they can produce statements that are unsupported by, or even contradictory to, retrieved evidence. This leads to hallucinations, difficulty incorporating up-to-date information, and failures to correctly interpret or reconcile sources. Recent work addresses these limitations by tightly coupling step-by-step reasoning with interactive search, treating retrieval as an action within an iterative agentic loop rather than a one-shot preprocessing step. This study reviews frameworks that implement such reasoning-search loops by interleaving planning, querying, retrieval, integration, verification, and stopping. We analyze these systems through four architectural axes: retrieval coupling, controller design, knowledge source, and grounding strictness. We separately treat the learning signal as a cross-cutting dimension because prompting, supervision, preference optimization, and reinforcement learning can instantiate the same architecture while producing materially different search, stopping, and grounding behavior. We further distinguish benchmark difficulty from RSA-loop demand and synthesize controlled within-study ablations and quality-cost deltas, avoiding invalid pooling across heterogeneous model backbones, retrievers, corpora, and evaluation protocols. Our goal is to clarify the landscape of reasoning-search-augmented LLMs and outline a unified perspective on the design choices shaping future research. | Biswas Poudel, Nilson Chapagain, Amit Kumar, Xianshun Jiang |
| Generative Recommenders Forget the Long Tail: Restoring Memorization in Retrieval and Ranking | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29298) | Generative recommenders replace the retrieve-then-rank cascade with a single model: beam search over semantic item identifiers performs retrieval, and the same likelihood performs ranking. We show that this shared score makes them forget long-tail items twice. In retrieval, beam search keeps identifier prefixes by the probability mass of their subtrees, so tail items are pruned early, and a wider beam admits few more of them. In ranking, the likelihood places tail items below head items even when they are admitted, so expanding retrieval alone brings little or no gain. Both failures reflect missing item-level memorization (i.e., direct recall of which items co-occur), which collaborative filtering provides and shared-prefix generation lacks. To this end, we propose CAPE (Co-occurrence-Aware Pool Expansion), a training-free plug-in that restores memorization at both stages: (i) a memorization channel retrieves co-occurrence-supported tail items outside the beam and scores them with the frozen generator, and (ii) memorization-calibrated ranking fuses the generator likelihood with co-occurrence evidence while keeping the generator's first item. Experiments with two generative backbones on product and music domains show that CAPE significantly improves long-tail accuracy over the backbones and over re-ranking baselines that share its candidates, preserves or improves overall accuracy, and adds a small fraction of the latency. | Xu Huang, Jiancan Wu, Hongzheng Qin, Jiawei Feng, Can Ye, Wencong Zeng, Fengze Li, Xiang Wang |
| Beyond the Vote: When Do LLMs Add Value to Retrieval-Based Tabular Classification? | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29520) | Retrieval-based in-context learning (ICL) enables large language models (LLMs) to predict tabular labels from similar examples, but it remains unclear whether LLM inference improves on direct voting over those same examples. We address this question by comparing four general-purpose LLMs with distance-weighted nearest-neighbor voting (Distance-KNN) using identical retrieved neighbors across 19 tabular classification datasets. With the number of demonstrations fixed, enlarging the retrieval candidate pool improves accuracy for all predictors, yet Distance-KNN achieves the highest mean accuracy at every pool size. More detailed analyses reveal that LLM gains are concentrated where retrieved neighbors exhibit low label consensus and vary across models. These gains further depend on the average feature-space distance to the test query: under low label consensus, some LLMs outperform Distance-KNN in close and intermediate neighborhoods, but all fall behind on average in the most distant group. Complementary experiments on the number of demonstrations, prompt formulation, retrieval representation, and label-balanced demonstration selection largely preserve the overall findings. These results characterize when LLM inference adds predictive value beyond distance-weighted voting in retrieval-based tabular ICL. | Ziquan Wang, Yuxuan Nie, Jian Ma, Yingjie Li |
| SIREN (Luring LLMs onto the Rocks): PAIR-Driven Preference Manipulation in Web-RAG Recommenders | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21951) | This paper investigates the adversarial manipulation of the ranked recommendations produced by web-augmented large language models (LLMs). When an LLM answers a recommendation query by retrieving and reading live webpages, it acts as a recommender, and each retrieved page becomes a potential attack surface. Prior work has examined fabricated products, retrieval poisoning, and rank promotion. However, these studies do not compare how different edits to an already retrieved page change the model's final ranking while the surrounding source set remains unchanged. To address this gap, we propose SIREN, an automated attacker--judge method that adapts the PAIR jailbreaking loop to competitive rank manipulation, with the goal of moving a chosen entity to rank~1 in an LLM-generated recommendation. SIREN retrieves and captures webpages using Anthropic's web tools, then iteratively edits a retrieved source using an interpretable taxonomy of 23 content-poisoning techniques. The custom-RAG replay platform keeps the same sources in the same order, so changes in the model's ranking can be linked to changes in the supplied content rather than to differences in retrieval. Across two production Claude models, SIREN reaches rank~1 in 62 of 124 technique trials nested within eight query--model contexts. The payloads that reached rank~1 were then tested in fresh sessions, where they reproduced the result with a mean success rate of 0.805. Across the evaluated settings, declarative ranking claims and seeded lists were generally more effective than directive-form injections, although the strength of this difference depended on the target model. To the best of our knowledge, this is among the first controlled studies of competitive rank manipulation in production LLMs where the supplied source context is kept fixed. | Evan Caville, Spencer Kayser, Siamak Layeghy, Billy Sung, Sara Dolnicar, Marius Portmann |
| WeGR: Towards Unified Generative Retrieval via Behavior-aware Tokenization and Preference Alignment | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16979) | Generative retrieval (GR) offers a promising paradigm for industrial search by unifying candidate generation within an autoregressive framework. However, effectively leveraging user behaviors in industrial GR remains challenging: existing SID tokenization methods struggle to preserve collaborative signals while maintaining balanced codebook utilization, whereas reinforcement learning (RL) for prioritizing high-value candidates suffers from sparse rewards when generated SIDs fail to match positive items. To address these challenges, we propose WeGR, a unified GR framework with two complementary components. Specifically, the Collaborative and Collapse-free Tokenizer (CoCo-VAE) injects query-to-item and item-to-item collaborative signals into continuous representations before quantization, preserving behavioral information without disrupting codebook optimization. It further couples first-layer codebook refresh to reactivate underutilized codewords with fidelity anti-collapse regularization, which broadens gradient coverage over relevant codewords and penalizes collisions in deeper layers to yield behavior-aware SIDs with balanced utilization. For preference alignment, Dynamic Horizon Policy Optimization (DHPO) progressively expands reward evaluation and policy optimization horizons to alleviate reward sparsity, enabling coarse-to-fine preference learning from SID prefixes to complete items without auxiliary reward models. Extensive offline experiments validate that WeGR improves retrieval and ranking performance. Moreover, a six-week online A/B test on a large-scale e-commerce search platform achieves relative gains of 2.539% in orders, 1.508% in CTR, and 1.381% in CVR, while reducing CPU and GPU usage by 25.5% and 26.1%. These results highlight the effectiveness and efficiency of WeGR in industrial deployment, supporting multiple search scenarios and tens of millions of daily page views. | Jiajun Han, Guoxin Ma,  Felix, Liangsheng Xu, Ruiqi Yu, Yingdi Guo, Zongbing Wang, Xinhua Ye, Chenyan Hua, Xin Xie, Pengcheng Yang, Haijun Wu |
| Controlling Output Rankings in LLM-Based Search via Generative Engine Optimization | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03608) | The way customers search for and choose products is changing with the rise of large language models (LLMs). LLM-based search, or generative engines, provides direct product recommendations to users, rather than traditional online search results that require users to explore options themselves. However, these recommendations are strongly influenced by the initial retrieval order of LLMs, which disadvantages small businesses and independent creators by limiting their visibility.
  In this work, we propose CORE, an optimization method that \textbf{C}ontrols \textbf{O}utput \textbf{R}ankings in g\textbf{E}nerative Engines for LLM-based search. Since the LLM's interactions with the search engine are black-box, CORE targets the content returned by search engines as the primary means of influencing output rankings. Specifically, CORE optimizes retrieved content by appending strategically designed optimization content to steer the ranking of outputs. We introduce three types of optimization content: string-based, reasoning-based, and review-based, demonstrating their effectiveness in shaping output rankings. To evaluate CORE in realistic settings, we introduce ProductBench, a large-scale benchmark with 15 product categories and 200 products per category, where each product is associated with its top-10 recommendations collected from Amazon's search interface.
  Extensive experiments on four LLMs with search capabilities (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, Claude-4, and Grok-3) demonstrate that CORE achieves an average Promotion Success Rate of \textbf{91.4\% @Top-5}, \textbf{86.6\% @Top-3}, and \textbf{80.3\% @Top-1}, across 15 product categories, outperforming existing ranking manipulation methods while preserving the fluency of optimized content. | Haibo Jin, Huimin Zeng, Yifeng Luo, Haohan Wang |
