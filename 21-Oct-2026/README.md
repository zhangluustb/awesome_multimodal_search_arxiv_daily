| Neighborhood-Aware Unlearning for Generative Recommendation | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27232) | Generative recommendation predicts items by autoregressively generating semantic identifiers (SIDs), whose tokens are shared across semantically related items. With this paradigm gaining adoption, the question of how to support unlearning in it has become pressing. If training interactions must later be removed, such as injected spam or interactions with items a user no longer wants, this sharing lets an unlearning update unintentionally affect the predictions for the interactions' neighbors. We show that this effect is locally structured and hidden by global metrics: unlearning can leave recommendation utility on the whole test set nearly unchanged while substantially degrading it on neighborhoods of the forgotten interactions' SIDs. We propose Neighborhood-Aware Unlearning (NAU), which augments interaction-level unlearning with explicit control over the probability mass assigned to the forgotten items' neighborhoods. Across three datasets and four SID tokenizers, NAU restores spam-induced exposure toward the retrained reference while retaining more than 95% of retrained utility, and recovers the neighborhood utility that forget-and-repair unlearning loses at no global cost. We identify local probability redistribution as an important failure mode of unlearning in SID-based generative recommenders and show that explicitly controlling this redistribution can mitigate its effects. | Pierre Lubitzsch, Yubao Tang, Maarten de Rijke, Sebastian Schelter |
| Multimodal Reranking Is a System One Decision | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28435) | Multimodal rerankers are usually dedicated models: a vision-language model (VLM) is trained on retrieval data to score the candidates of a query. Scoring options is not specific to reranking. System One decision models such as Jev map a state and a typed question to one probability per option without generating text, and general models can make such decisions with little or no training. Reranking is one such decision, with the query and its candidates as the state and the candidates as the options. We therefore argue that a general VLM can serve as a multimodal reranker through System One decisions, and that decision tuning can improve its reranking. We ask a VLM one choice question over the top 16 candidates of a first-stage retriever and rank the candidates by the probabilities of their labels, read from one forward pass. On MMEB-V2 image retrieval, this choice scores 5.4 to 7.3 hit@1 above a yes/no question per candidate, and the yes/no question falls below the first stage. With the choice, zero-shot Qwen3.8-27B beats the supervised Qwen3-VL-Reranker-2B on all three benchmarks and comes within 1.6 points of Reranker-8B on each. It matches Reranker-8B on MMEB-V2 visual documents (84.79 vs. 84.78 nDCG@5), at 1.1 to 1.5 times its latency. JEV-27B-VL, the same backbone tuned on text decisions only, improves page reranking by 0.6 to 1.1 points, with gains on most tasks (sign test, p <= 0.017), and leaves natural images unchanged. The zero-shot model is larger than the supervised rerankers and of a newer generation, so these comparisons are confounded; we discuss this and other alternative views, and the experiments that would test the position. | Zhuchenyang Liu, Yao Zhang, Yu Xiao |
| GARRET: Generative Feedback for Region Retrieval in Visual Documents | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829) | Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents requires evidence at a finer granularity than entire pages. Existing visual retrievers typically index or return full pages, whereas training region-level retrievers often requires costly query-to-region annotations or noisy model-generated labels. We introduce GARRET, a region-retrieval framework trained from (page, question, answer) triplets through differentiable generative feedback. A shared context-preserving retriever produces page and region multi-vector representations. During training, their retrieval scores define a soft routing distribution over candidate region crops. An auxiliary vision-language model attends to each crop separately, aggregates crop-specific contexts using the routing weights, and predicts the question followed by the answer under a next-token prediction objective. Gradients through the routing distribution assign query-conditioned credit to the indexed region representations, without requiring query-to-region annotations. At inference, the auxiliary model is removed. GARRET also reuses the contextualized region representations, together with page summary tokens, to construct a compact page representation. We introduce ViDoRe-V3-Region, a corpus-wide benchmark containing 200,682 fixed candidate regions across eight domains. GARRET achieves a macro nDCG@10 of 0.4126, outperforming the strongest visual baseline by 2.94 points. On page retrieval, it remains comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing 47.8% fewer vectors per page. | Weijian Jian, Siyang Chen, Yuguang Yang, Yajun Zhang, Chunyu Xie, Dawei Leng, Yuhui Yin |
| Evolving Semantic Indexes for Generative Retrieval | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28568) | Generative retrieval (GR) casts search and recommendation as autoregressive generation of discrete semantic identifiers (SIDs). SID design largely determines GR quality, but its discrete, interdependent components (SID construction, collision handling, decoding, and training) cannot be optimized end-to-end with gradients and remain largely hand-designed. We introduce a framework that improves these components through LLM-driven evolutionary program search, to our knowledge the first application of this approach to GR. A locally served, open-weight 31B model mutates executable GR components, so no proprietary LLM API is used, and a 1M-parameter proxy decoder provides fast feedback in place of repeatedly training the 1B target generator. Across 21 SID programs on NQ320K, proxy and target Recall@100 show strong rank correlation (Spearman $\rho = 0.90$). Guided by this proxy, evolution improves the seed GR on NQ320K, MSMARCO300K, and a multilingual e-commerce dataset under a fixed training recipe. On NQ320K, jointly evolving SID construction and collision handling improves Recall@1 from 55.8% to 59.3% and Recall@100 from 85.8% to 90.6%. A component study shows that proxy reliability depends on what is evolved: improvements transfer reliably for the semantic index but not for the generator's training objective or the full pipeline. LLM-driven evolutionary search is thus a practical tool for GR design, provided proxy reliability is established for the chosen search space. | Jinming Nian, Jyun-Yu Jiang, Henry Tsai, Yi Fang, Zhirong Liang |
| WeGR: Towards Unified Generative Retrieval via Behavior-aware Tokenization and Preference Alignment | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16979) | Generative retrieval (GR) offers a promising paradigm for industrial search by unifying candidate generation within an autoregressive framework. However, effectively leveraging user behaviors in industrial GR remains challenging: existing SID tokenization methods struggle to preserve collaborative signals while maintaining balanced codebook utilization, whereas reinforcement learning (RL) for prioritizing high-value candidates suffers from sparse rewards when generated SIDs fail to match positive items. To address these challenges, we propose WeGR, a unified GR framework with two complementary components. Specifically, the Collaborative and Collapse-free Tokenizer (CoCo-VAE) injects query-to-item and item-to-item collaborative signals into continuous representations before quantization, preserving behavioral information without disrupting codebook optimization. It further couples first-layer codebook refresh to reactivate underutilized codewords with fidelity anti-collapse regularization, which broadens gradient coverage over relevant codewords and penalizes collisions in deeper layers to yield behavior-aware SIDs with balanced utilization. For preference alignment, Dynamic Horizon Policy Optimization (DHPO) progressively expands reward evaluation and policy optimization horizons to alleviate reward sparsity, enabling coarse-to-fine preference learning from SID prefixes to complete items without auxiliary reward models. Extensive offline experiments validate that WeGR improves retrieval and ranking performance. Moreover, a six-week online A/B test on a large-scale e-commerce search platform achieves relative gains of 2.539% in orders, 1.508% in CTR, and 1.381% in CVR, while reducing CPU and GPU usage by 25.5% and 26.1%. These results highlight the effectiveness and efficiency of WeGR in industrial deployment, supporting multiple search scenarios and tens of millions of daily page views. | Jiajun Han, Guoxin Ma,  Felix, Liangsheng Xu, Ruiqi Yu, Yingdi Guo, Zongbing Wang, Xinhua Ye, Chenyan Hua, Xin Xie, Pengcheng Yang, Haijun Wu |
