| VoteGEO-Bench: Measuring the Impact of Generative Engine Optimization on Electoral Visibility | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21330) | Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for election information, yet little is known about how external web content shapes the political information they present. Generative engine optimization (GEO) rewrites web content to improve its visibility in generated responses. To investigate its potential risks in voting scenarios (VoteGEO), we introduce VoteGEO-Bench, a benchmark covering the 2024 U.S. presidential election and the 2026 Japanese House of Representatives election, combining election-related queries, historical webpages, and voter personas. We first analyze sources cited by generative engines and find that many citations come from lower-cost owned and open-platform sources, while some webpages remain visible across days. We then evaluate 11 rewriting strategies in 4 categories, examining target specification, effectiveness across engines and elections, and the role of voter personas. Rewriting without specifying a target rarely increases target exposure, whereas target-specified rewriting more often does. Strategies that improve page organization or strengthen a position through argumentation yield greater gains than adding supporting evidence or changing wording. For the two strongest strategies, gains remain broadly positive across evaluated U.S. personas, but persona conditioning shows no consistent effect on these gains. Explicit partisan identities can create baseline exposure gaps larger than the gain from rewriting one webpage. When partisanship is implicit in the voter profile, baseline shifts depend on how strongly the engine responds to that signal. | Shiqi Zhang, Xiaokui Xiao |
| Aligning LLMs with Sparse Retrieval via Adaptive Sparse Supervision | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21877) | While large language models (LLMs) offer strong semantic representations, their application to first-stage retrieval incurs substantial latency and storage costs. Neural sparse retrievers such as SPLADE provide efficient retrieval but rely on relatively compact encoders with limited representational capacity. To bridge this gap, we propose an LLM-based sparse retrieval framework with input-adaptive sparse alignment. Specifically, we introduce a dynamic $\alpha$-truncation strategy that selects input-specific supervision supports based on the cumulative activation mass of SPLADE representations. This strategy guides the LLM to preserve salient lexical signals while suppressing less informative activations, without relying on a fixed support budget. Subsequent retrieval fine-tuning combines contrastive learning, knowledge distillation, and sparsity regularization to improve retrieval effectiveness. Experiments on MS MARCO and six selected BEIR datasets demonstrate improvements over SPLADE++ while maintaining competitive effectiveness against LLM-based sparse retrievers. Compared with Lion-sp, our method reduces index storage by 6.4-fold and end-to-end query latency by over 11-fold, achieving a favorable effectiveness-efficiency trade-off. | Tongna Chen, Minghan Li, Guodong Zhou |
| SSR: Structural Signal Retriever for Scalable Agent Discovery in LLM-Orchestrated Multi-Agent Systems | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21113) | Large-scale multi-agent ecosystems require mechanisms for discovering suitable agents without exposing entire registries to a large language model (LLM) orchestrator. The challenge is not only to retrieve relevant Agent Cards from a growing registry, but also to maintain a compact candidate context, since each additional candidate increases prompt size, inference cost, latency, and the risk of exceeding the model context window. We formulate this problem as candidate-context scalability and propose the Structural Signal Retriever (SSR), our deterministic and training-free retrieval method designed for Agent Card discovery. SSR derives structural lexical signals from capability-bearing Agent Card fields, weights them using inverse document frequency (IDF), and uses an Inverted Signal Index (ISI) for selective candidate scoring. We evaluate SSR using a synthetic agent benchmark, a public MS MARCO adaptation, and end-to-end LLM orchestration. At 1,000 agents, SSR reduces LLM input by 98.8% and orchestration latency by 70.9% relative to full-catalog orchestration. On MS MARCO, IDF improves retrieval effectiveness within the structural representation, while the ISI preserves retrieval effectiveness and reduces explicitly scored passages by 96.8%. End-to-end experiments show that candidate pre-selection maintains a compact candidate context as registry size grows and avoids the operational failures observed with full-catalog orchestration. These results support SSR as an Agent-Card-native, traceable retrieval mechanism for candidate-context scalability. | Giancarlo Maldonado C\'ardenas, Carlos A. Astudillo, Nelson L. S. da Fonseca |
| TRACER: Trajectory-Aware Tool Retrieval for LLM Agents | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21267) | Modern agents select tools from registries, but exposing entire registries incurs high token costs and can impair selection as they grow. We present TRACER, a retrieval front-end that indexes observed tool-call sequences in a trie and ranks continuations using a learned blend of empirical and contextual priors. On a mixed retail-and-telecom $\tau^2$ environment, TRACER improves GPT-5.4's tool-selection accuracy by 4.7 points while reducing input tokens per step by 80%, exposing just 5 of 128 tools. In autonomous full-turn execution, it matches GPT-5.4's full-registry task success with 61% fewer input tokens per step. Across six environments and four models, TRACER improves tool-selection accuracy over full-registry selection in 21 of 24 model-environment pairs, with average gains of 6 to 10 points; the three exceptions trail by at most 2.2. We analyze failures in both regimes to identify directions for future work. | Tanish Kher, Sarvesh DK, Abedelkadir Asi, Sparsha Chennarayapatna Sundaresh, Dezhi Hong |
| Retrieved but Not Read: Understanding Why Search-Augmented LLMs Fail to Use Available Evidence | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21731) | Web search allows LLMs to access up-to-date information, but it remains unclear why they sometimes produce outdated answers even when relevant evidence is available. To study this problem, we construct temporal facts whose values changed after the models' training cutoffs. We evaluate one proprietary model under deployed web search and six open-weight model configurations under controlled retrieval. We find that models often answer from search snippets without opening the source pages, which is associated with higher rates of outdated answers and unfaithful citations. Our controlled experiments and layer-wise analysis reveal a gap between the availability of evidence and its effective use. Even when retrieved evidence influences internal predictions, models may still favour outdated information in their final answers. We hope these findings will help advance research on the reliable use of retrieved evidence in search-augmented LLMs. | Owen Walters, Lihu Chen, Francesca Toni |
| How Do LLM-Generated Texts Impact Term-Based Retrieval Models? | [https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17715) | As more content generated by large language models (LLMs) floods into the Internet, information retrieval (IR) systems now face the challenge of distinguishing and handling a blend of human-authored and machine-generated texts. Recent studies suggest that neural retrievers may exhibit a preferential inclination toward LLM-generated content, while classic term-based retrievers like BM25 tend to favor human-written documents. This paper investigates the influence of LLM-generated content on term-based retrieval models, which are valued for their efficiency and robust generalization across domains. Our linguistic analysis reveals that LLM-generated texts exhibit smoother high-frequency and steeper low-frequency Zipf slopes, higher term specificity, and greater document-level diversity. These traits are aligned with LLMs being trained to optimize reader experience through diverse and precise expressions. Our study further explores whether term-based retrieval models demonstrate source bias, concluding that these models prioritize documents whose term distributions closely correspond to those of the queries, rather than displaying an inherent source bias. This work provides a foundation for understanding and addressing potential biases in term-based IR systems managing mixed-source content. | Wei Huang, Keping Bi, Yinqiong Cai, Wei Chen, Jiafeng Guo, Xueqi Cheng |
